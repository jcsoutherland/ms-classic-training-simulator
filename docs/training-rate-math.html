How the training rates are worked out

Every number the simulator shows comes from the steps below, in this order. Each step says where its numbers come from.

Game filesread from MapleStory Classic's own data NiaDBNiaDB's formulas page or class guides Assumptiona modelling choice, not a game value Your logsmeasured from mapleclassic.training sessions

1How often you hit

Your accuracy against the monster's evasion, with a penalty when the monster is higher level.

L = max(0, monster level − your level) A = ACC × 100 / (10L + 255) f = 0.15 + 0.20 / (1 + e^((A − EVA) / 12)), q = EVA / A hit chance = 1 if q ≤ 1 − f, 0 if q ≥ 1 + f, otherwise (1 + f − q) / 2f

NiaDBformula. Game filesmonster level and evasion. Your accuracy comes from your Stats window.

2How much each hit does

Start from the attack range in your Stats window, multiply by the skill's damage, then by the monster's defense, the level gap and any element weakness. Crits use the crit rate and crit damage from your Stats window's Detail panel.

hit = (lowest..highest attack) × skill % × 100 / (DEF + 100) × level gap (1 / (1 + gap² × 0.005) below 10 levels) × element (weak 1.25, resist 0.75, immune 0) × (1 + crit rate × (crit damage − 100%)) spell = MagicTotal × spell % × (1 + INT × mastery / 125 … 1 + INT / 125)

NiaDBformulas. Game filesskill damage per level, monster defense and elements (the Jr. Wraith is weak to holy and fire). Heal on undead uses the Cleric guide's formula: Pool = ((INT × R + LUK) / 1000 + 2.5) × MagicTotal × R, damage per monster = Pool × (1 + 0.5(t − 1)) / t / 1.5, with t = 3 monsters assumed.

3How many hits a kill takes

The old model rounded average damage up to whole hits, so a small damage boost changed nothing. Now it uses the expected number of hits when each hit lands somewhere between your lowest and highest damage, then divides by your hit chance.

expected hits ≈ HP / average hit + (1 + spread²) / 2 spread² = (highest − lowest)² / 12 / average² kept between ⌈HP / highest⌉ and ⌈HP / lowest⌉ attempts = expected hits / hit chance

Assumptionthis is the standard renewal-theory estimate for how many random steps it takes to pass a total. It stays exact at both ends: one-hit kills stay one hit.

4How long each attack takes

attack time = Σ frame delays × (10 + speed tier) / 16, rounded up to 30 ms

Game filesbody animation frames and weapon speed. NiaDBspeed tiers, booster −2 tiers, spells at tier 6.

5How monsters come back

This was the biggest missing piece. A defeated monster doesn't reappear where it died. Every respawn tick, the map refills up to its capacity at random empty spawn points anywhere on the map, so the platform you're on empties out and you have to move.

tick every 7.56 s; a spawn point waits 5 s after its monster dies capacity = spawn points × min(1, 0.75 + 0.05 × (players − 1))

NiaDBtick, cooldown and capacity. Game filesevery spawn point's position. Assumptionrefilling at random empty points follows how MapleStory server emulators do it.

6Getting around the map

walk = 125 × (speed + 20) / 100 px/s, climb = speed + 20 px/s drop down a floor = 0.25 s + height / 670 px/s

NiaDBwalk and climb speeds. Game filesropes, ladders and platforms; routes go up only by rope and down by dropping. Assumptiondrop-down time.

7Monsters wandering

Monsters don't stand on their spawn points. Every spawn point has a stretch of platform its monster walks along, and 107 of the 117 Classic monsters have a walking or flying animation. When and where a monster turns is decided by the game server, so it isn't in the game files. The simulator uses the long-run result of that walking instead: at every respawn tick, each walking monster is placed somewhere along its stretch with equal chance. A monster you're already fighting stays put.

monster x = wander left + random × (wander right − wander left), redrawn every 7.56 s

Game fileseach spawn point's wander range and which monsters have a move or fly animation. Assumptionan even spread along the range.

It changes little: 1 to 5% less EXP for single-target attacks, and about 10% less for attacks that hit several monsters in a line, because spread-out monsters bunch up less often.

8The training session itself

The simulator plays a fixed-seed 15-minute session on the real map, after a one-minute warm-up, and repeats it once for each monster you could camp. It keeps whichever gives the most EXP an hour.

  • You go after the nearest monster of your chosen type. Other monsters only get hit when they're already in range, the way you'd hit something in the way.
  • A multi-target skill only hits monsters actually within its reach on the same platform, up to its target count.
  • In a party, members can't take the same monster, so six players share and crowd the same spawns.

Game filesskill reach, hit boxes and target counts. Assumptionthis targeting behaviour.

9Picking up drops

A moderately efficient player loots most kills. Ranged jobs kill from further away, so looting costs them more walking than melee jobs.

per kill: 85% × (walk to the corpse + 0.3 s per monster looted) items and mesos you pick up = 85% of what drops

Assumption85% and 0.3 s, set to match "moderately efficient" as you described. Your log's Tablecloth and Jr. Necki Skin counts show about 80% picked up.

10EXP and mesos

EXP each = monster EXP, split in a party by NiaDB's share and party bonus mesos per kill = 67% chance × average of the monster's meso range × 85% picked up

Game filesmonster EXP. NiaDBparty share and meso ranges. Your logsthe 67% chance (NiaDB's community reports say 100%, which your logs contradict).
